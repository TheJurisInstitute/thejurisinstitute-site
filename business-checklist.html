The LLC & Contract
Checklist for Business Owners

12 steps to protect your business when you form an LLC and before you sign any contract, plus three habits that keep you protected.

The Juris Institute — 25+ Years of Legal Experience

Most business legal problems start small: a missing operating agreement, a contract signed in the owner’s own name, a vague scope of work, a missed annual report. Each one is easy to prevent and expensive to fix later. Use this checklist when you form your LLC and every time someone hands you a contract to sign. It is general education, not legal advice, and requirements vary by state.

Forming and Running Your LLC

01
Choose your state of formation on purpose
Most small businesses are best served by forming in the state where they actually operate. Forming in Delaware, Wyoming or Nevada usually means registering as a foreign LLC at home too, which adds a second set of fees and annual reports. Decide before you file, not after.
Common costly mistake
02
Confirm your business name is available, and protect it
Search your state’s business entity database, then check federal trademark records and the domain name you want. A name your state accepts can still infringe someone else’s trademark.
03
Appoint a reliable registered agent
Your registered agent receives lawsuits and official state mail. If that person or company misses a notice, a default judgment or an administrative dissolution can follow. Use a stable address that is staffed during business hours.
04
Sign an operating agreement, even for a one-member LLC
The operating agreement sets ownership percentages, voting, how profits are split, and what happens if a member leaves, becomes disabled or dies. Without one, your state’s default rules decide for you, and a single-member LLC without one is easier to attack in court.
Do not skip this step
05
Get an EIN and open a separate business bank account
An Employer Identification Number from the IRS is free. Run every business dollar through the business account. Mixing personal and business money is one of the main reasons courts “pierce the veil” and reach an owner’s personal assets.
06
Calendar your annual report, licenses and tax deadlines
Most states require an annual or biennial report and fee. Miss it and the state can dissolve your LLC. Add local business licenses, sales tax permits and estimated tax payments to the same calendar.

Before You Sign Any Contract

07
Make sure the right parties are named, and sign as the LLC
The contract should name your LLC by its exact registered name, and you should sign as “Member” or “Manager” of the LLC. Signing in your own name can make you personally liable for the business’s obligations.
Common costly mistake
08
Pin down the scope of work and the deliverables
Vague scope causes most contract disputes. Spell out exactly what will be delivered, by when, in what form, and what counts as complete. Anything not written down is open to argument later.
09
Spell out payment terms and what happens if someone pays late
State the price, due dates, accepted payment methods, deposits, late fees and whether you can pause work for nonpayment. Confirm any late fee or interest rate complies with your state’s limits.
10
Read the termination, liability and indemnification clauses twice
These clauses decide who pays when something goes wrong. Look for a way out of the contract, a cap on your liability, and whether you are agreeing to cover the other side’s losses or legal fees.
Read twice
11
Check who owns the work and who must keep things confidential
For creative work, software, content or designs, the contract should say who owns the intellectual property and when ownership transfers. Add confidentiality terms if either side will share sensitive information.
12
Know where and how disputes will be resolved
Look for the governing law, the location of any lawsuit, and whether disputes go to arbitration. A clause requiring you to litigate in another state can make a small claim too expensive to pursue.

Bonus: Habits That Keep You Protected

A
Keep a signed copy of every agreement in one place
Store the fully signed version, every amendment and key emails in a single folder for each client or vendor. You cannot enforce what you cannot find.
B
Put every change in writing
A phone call that changes the price or the deadline is hard to prove. Confirm every change by email at minimum, or with a short written amendment both sides sign.
C
Review your agreements once a year
Your business changes. Review your operating agreement and your standard contracts each year, or whenever you add a member, a new service or a new state.
