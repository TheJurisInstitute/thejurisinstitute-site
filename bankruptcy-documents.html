Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Bankruptcy Documents

What is the difference between Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 bankruptcy?

Chapter 7 bankruptcy is a liquidation bankruptcy — a trustee is appointed to sell non-exempt assets and distribute the proceeds to creditors. Most unsecured debts (credit cards, medical bills, personal loans) can be discharged in Chapter 7, often within 4 to 6 months. Chapter 13 bankruptcy is a reorganization bankruptcy — you propose a 3 to 5 year repayment plan to repay some or all of your debts, while keeping your assets. Chapter 13 is often used by people who have assets they want to protect, have income that exceeds the Chapter 7 means test, or want to save a home from foreclosure. Both have specific eligibility requirements.

What documents are required to file for bankruptcy?

A bankruptcy filing requires a comprehensive set of documents and official forms, including: a completed Voluntary Petition (Form 101), schedules of all assets (Schedule A/B), all liabilities and creditors (Schedules D, E/F), current income (Schedule I), monthly expenses (Schedule J), a Statement of Financial Affairs, a Statement of Intention for secured debts, a means test calculation (Form 122A or 122C), certificate of credit counseling, and supporting financial documents including recent tax returns, pay stubs, and bank statements. We help you organize and prepare these documents based on the financial information you provide.

What does The Juris Institute prepare for bankruptcy filings?

The Juris Institute prepares bankruptcy petition documents and schedules based on the financial information and instructions you provide. We organize the official forms required for your filing, format them to court specifications, and prepare the accompanying schedules based on the asset, liability, income, and expense information you supply. We do not provide legal advice regarding which chapter to file, whether to file at all, or how specific debts or assets will be treated by the bankruptcy court — these are legal strategy questions that require a licensed bankruptcy attorney.

Will bankruptcy stop a foreclosure?

Filing for bankruptcy triggers an automatic stay — an immediate court order that halts most collection actions, including foreclosure proceedings, garnishments, repossessions, and creditor lawsuits. The automatic stay provides temporary breathing room, but it does not eliminate secured debts or permanently stop foreclosure. In a Chapter 13, you can use the repayment plan to catch up on mortgage arrears and potentially save your home if you maintain ongoing mortgage payments. In a Chapter 7, the stay is temporary and the lender can seek relief from the stay to proceed with foreclosure. If foreclosure is your primary concern, see also our Foreclosure Defense Documents service.

Do I need an attorney to file for bankruptcy?

Individuals are legally permitted to file for bankruptcy without an attorney — a process called filing pro se. Many people do file their own bankruptcy petitions, particularly straightforward Chapter 7 cases. However, bankruptcy law is complex, and mistakes in your petition or schedules can have serious consequences, including denial of discharge. The Juris Institute prepares the documents based on what you tell us, but we do not provide legal advice about your case. For complex situations — significant assets, business debts, prior filings, or potential fraud allegations — a licensed bankruptcy attorney is strongly advisable.

How long does a bankruptcy filing remain on my credit report?

A Chapter 7 bankruptcy remains on your credit report for 10 years from the filing date. A Chapter 13 bankruptcy remains on your credit report for 7 years from the filing date. These timelines are set by the Fair Credit Reporting Act. While the bankruptcy notation itself is a significant negative mark, many people find that their credit scores begin to recover within 1 to 2 years of discharge, particularly if they establish new positive credit history. The long-term credit impact should be weighed against the financial relief bankruptcy may provide.
