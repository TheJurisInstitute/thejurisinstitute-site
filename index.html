Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is legal document preparation and how is it different from legal advice?

Legal document preparation is the professional preparation of legal documents based on the facts and instructions provided by the client. A document preparer organizes, drafts, and formats the documents — but does not advise you on what legal decisions to make, what strategy to pursue, or what outcome to expect. Legal advice involves applying the law to your specific facts and making strategic recommendations — that is the practice of law, which requires bar licensure. At The Juris Institute, we prepare the documents you direct us to create, explain court processes and procedures in plain terms, and provide legal education — without crossing into legal advice or representation.

How much does it cost? Is there a retainer or hourly billing?

There are no retainers, no hourly billing, and no billing surprises. Every project is quoted at a flat fee before any work begins. You know the full price upfront and pay that amount — nothing more. Flat-fee pricing means you can budget for your document preparation cost with certainty, unlike traditional attorney billing where the final invoice can be far higher than the initial estimate. We provide a free flat-fee quote within one business day of receiving your inquiry.

How fast can I receive my documents?

Turnaround time varies by project complexity, but most single-document projects are completed within 2 to 5 business days. Urgent matters — particularly litigation documents with pending deadlines — are given priority and may be completed in 24 to 48 hours. When you submit your inquiry, please include any relevant deadlines so we can prioritize accordingly. We confirm your timeline and flat-fee quote before any work begins.

What states do you serve?

We serve clients in all 50 states and Washington D.C. The Juris Institute operates entirely virtually — all communication is in writing by email, ensuring a complete written record of every matter. Documents are delivered electronically, and there is no requirement to travel to an office or appear in person at any point. We prepare documents formatted to your state's specific requirements.

Do I need to have a lawyer review my documents after you prepare them?

You are always responsible for reviewing any documents you sign, execute, or file. Whether to have a licensed attorney review your documents before use is a decision only you can make. For straightforward documents in common legal matters, many clients use our prepared documents without additional attorney review. For complex, high-stakes matters — contested litigation, large business transactions, or matters involving significant assets — independent legal counsel may be advisable.

What if I need revisions after I receive my documents?

Revisions within the scope of your original project are included at no additional charge. If you provide new information, change your instructions, or need corrections, we make those revisions as part of your flat fee. Additional work beyond the original scope would be quoted separately before any work begins.
